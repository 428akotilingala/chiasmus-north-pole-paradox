# Presenter guide: Classroom Quick Play

About 7 minutes. Two presenters, one keyboard, the whole class voting by raising hands.

- **Presenter 1 (P1), the Host:** drives the keyboard, voices Santa and the Syntax Grinch, runs the votes.
- **Presenter 2 (P2), the Lead Investigator:** explains the device, gives hints, reads the debriefs.

## Before class (2 minutes)

1. Open https://428akotilingala.github.io/chiasmus-north-pole-paradox/ on the projector computer.
   Once the page loads it needs no internet, so a Wi-Fi drop mid-presentation is fine.
2. Click once anywhere so sound can start, then press **F** for fullscreen.
3. Optional: **Settings › Class vote timer** (10 s is the default; choose Off for no time pressure).
4. Click **CLASSROOM QUICK PLAY**.

## Keys you'll use

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| Enter / Space / click | Next line, next panel, continue |
| 1–4 | Choose answer A–D (or click it) |
| N | Presenter notes for this screen (the answer key stays hidden until you click "Show answer key") |
| Esc | Pause, restart the chapter, or jump to any chapter |
| WASD / arrows, R | Move in the maze; R flips reality on a glowing mirror |
| M | Mute |

Nothing advances on its own. Every class question waits until you click.

## Timed script

| Time | Screen | P1 | P2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | **Grammar emergency** (5 panels) | Read Santa's lines in a panicked Santa voice; click through. | "We're the N.R.I.D. Our job: find elves misusing reversal." |
| 0:30 | **What is chiasmus?** | Ask: "What do you notice about where the words go?" | "Chiasmus reverses parallel parts: A-B, then B-A. Draw the lines and they cross, like the Greek letter chi. Antimetabole is the version with the exact same words. Same order twice, or plain repetition, is NOT chiasmus." |
| 1:00 | **Case 1** | Read the four statements (or pick four volunteers). Click **Call the class vote**: "Hands up for A… B… C… D." | 2:00 | **Case 2** | "Everyone repeats this time. Find the one where nothing swaps." Run the vote. | After the reveal, ask the discussion question: "Why is a reversal more memorable than repetition?" Click **Reveal an answer**. |
| 3:00 | **Mirror Maze** | Drive, or hand the keyboard to a volunteer. Walk to the glowing mirror, press **R**. | "Reality A-B, then Mirror B-A. Gold doors open in one, cyan in the other. The world itself is doing chiasmus." Each gate pauses for a class question. |
| 5:30 | **The Reality Mirror** | Voice the Syntax Grinch. Lean into "A-A-A-A!" | Let the class say where each block goes: matching pairs go on the outside and the inside. Then run "What does this reversal emphasize?" |
| 6:30 | **Ending** | Deliver the closing line: "We made a game about chiasmus, and chiasmus made the game." | Read the four reasons on **WHY THIS GAME IS CHIASMUS**. Point classmates to **Kennedy examples** for their organizers. |

## Answer key

### Case 1: The Self-Unwrapping Presents (basic A-B / B-A)

**Impostor: C, Juniper Jinglesworth.** "Santa feeds the reindeer, and Santa thanks the reindeer."
A-B / A-B: Santa stays first and the reindeer stay second. Parallelism, no swap.

Real examples: A "Santa trusts the elves, and the elves trust Santa." B "We don't forget the cookies,
and the cookies don't forget us." D "Keep the snow on the roof, not the roof in the snow."

### Case 2: The Repetition Infection (chiasmus vs. repetition and parallelism)

**Impostor: B, Mistle Toby (Sir Repeats-a-Lot in disguise).** "We wrap for the children, we bake for
the children, we sing for the children!" Repetition (A … A … A); nothing trades places.

Real examples: A "Elves should work to live, not live to work." C "You can take the elf out of the
workshop, but you can't take the workshop out of the elf." D "Naughty can turn nice, and nice can
turn naughty."

### Case 3 (Adventure only): The Clock Tower Conspiracy (reversed grammar and ideas)

**Impostor: D, Flurry Featherfrost.** "Hope for snow, but prepare for slush." Antithesis in the same
order (action-thing / action-thing), so nothing crosses.

Real examples: A "We lost the sleigh, but our hope we kept." B "By day we wrap the gifts; the carols
we sing by night." C "The workshop is warm; cold is the tundra."

### Maze gates

| Level | Gate | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quick Play | The elves protect Christmas, and Christmas protects ____. | A: the elves |
| Quick Play | Why is this more memorable than ordinary repetition? | A: the swap shows a two-way relationship in one balanced, quotable sentence |
| Maze 1 | The elves protect Christmas… / We warm the cocoa, and the cocoa warms ____. | A / A (us) |
| Maze 2 | …we don't play with the toys; ____ / We don't wrap gifts to be happy; ____ | A (the toys play with us) / A (we're happy to wrap gifts) |
| Maze 2 secret | Great teams build great players, and great players build great ____. | A: teams |
| Maze 3 | By day, the clocks tick forward; ____ / We don't light the tower because it's Christmas… | A (backward they tick by night) / A (Christmas is something people create) |

### Finale

Build **You / Christmas / Christmas / you** ("You didn't save Christmas — Christmas saved you."), or
**Christmas / you / you / Christmas**. Both are valid chiasmus; the game explains that the first ends
on gratitude and the second on responsibility. Emphasis question: **A**, the relationship runs both
ways.

## Maze tips

- Stand on a glowing round mirror and press **R** to flip between Reality A-B and Mirror B-A.
- Gold **A** doors open in A-B; cyan **B** doors and ice bridges exist in B-A.
- Walk into a red χ gate to open the class question.
- If anything gets stuck, **Backspace** resets the room, and **Esc › Restart chapter** restarts the level.
